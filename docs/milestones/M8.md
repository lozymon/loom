# M8 — Voice

**Goal:** talk to Loom and hear it back, in English and Brazilian Portuguese, from the browser client at home and at work.

**Exit criteria**

- Hold a button or `Ctrl+Shift+Space` in the client, speak, release: the transcript lands in the target's composer as editable text, sent with Enter. Trailing "send" or "enviar" sends it only when auto-send is on.
- Speech is transcribed on a hub by local whisper.cpp (`sidecars/voce`) or Groq, whichever the hub has. English and Portuguese are told apart per utterance.
- With the approvals panel open, "allow", "deny", "permitir", or "negar" decides the selected approval. Approvals on the always-ask list are never decided by voice.
- The client reads aloud, when enabled: approvals waiting for a person, sessions finishing or failing, and `speak` calls. Never tool output. Read-back uses the browser's voice, or the hub's (OpenAI or Piper) when chosen, with the voice matching the text's language.
- Sessions can call a `speak` tool (and `loom say`); what they say is shown in the timeline as well.
- One mute switch, one stop key, per-hub quiet hours.

## Design decisions

- **Audio is captured in the client and recognized on a hub.** The browser has the microphone but cannot run whisper; the hub runs whisper.cpp or holds the Groq key. The client records with `MediaRecorder`, decodes and resamples to 16 kHz mono in the browser, and sends a PCM WAV. No ffmpeg on the hub. The desktop app (M9) uses the same path.
- **The whisper sidecar is optional,** like the terminal sidecar. It loads its model on first use and exits after 10 idle minutes to free memory. The hub never downloads models; it says where to put one.
- **Language:** whisper's language probabilities are compared for English and Portuguese only, then the clip is decoded in the winner. Text language for read-back is a small word-list classifier shared in `@loom/protocol`.
- **Keys stay on the hub,** read from `GROQ_API_KEY` and `OPENAI_API_KEY`, never from `hub.json` or the client.
- **Read-back is a client setting** (per browser), applied to live events only, never to replayed history. Quiet hours come from the hub and use the client's clock.
- **`speak` is a session event** (`speech`), so it is in the log and the timeline: voice never has information the screen lacks.

## Verification (2026-09-13)

- Tests: 297 passing, 2 skipped (live SDK), plus 3 Rust unit tests in `sidecars/voce`. New: WAV parsing and limits, the whisper sidecar client against a fake sidecar (order, per-clip errors, crash and restart, load failure, idle stop), Groq and OpenAI against a local HTTP server (key, form fields, language mapping, prompt), Piper with a fake command, engine selection, `speak` permissions and its event, language detection and quiet hours, dictation and approval words, read-back rules, WAV encoding.
- Sidecar alone on this machine (i5-12400F, CPU build): the whisper.cpp JFK sample transcribed correctly as English with `small` (5.5 s, 3.0 s with the language fixed) and `medium` (18 s, 9.5 s). A Piper pt_BR sentence was detected as Portuguese; one word was misheard with `small` ("Rode" → "Pode").
- Browser, against a hub on 7421 with whisper `small` and Piper configured, with `getUserMedia` replaced by a stream playing sample WAVs (the automation browser has no real microphone input):
  - Holding `Ctrl+Shift+Space` with the Portuguese clip (22.05 kHz, so resampled in the browser): "Pode todos os testes do projeto e depois me diga quantos falharam." landed in Faye's message box, badge PT, cursor in the box.
  - The 11 s English clip: transcribed correctly, badge EN.
  - A `speak` from Faye in Portuguese showed as "🔊 …" in the timeline and played a Piper WAV from the hub; `voice.speak` chose the pt voice.
  - A Haiku session asked to `touch` a file: the approval was read aloud through Piper. With the approvals panel open, a Piper "Allow." clip was first heard as "below." and refused as not a command. After adding the expected-words prompt and a 0.7 s lead-in, it was heard as "Allow." and allowed the request, and the file appeared. Cost $0.01.
  - Escape during a 10 s read-back stopped it.
- Not verified: a real microphone, the browser's own voice (this browser has none installed), Groq and OpenAI with real keys, whisper on Windows, the CUDA build, the always-ask refusal and target "the Cockpit" in the browser (unit-level only).
